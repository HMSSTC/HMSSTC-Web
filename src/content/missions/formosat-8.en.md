## Expanding the boundaries of space observation, from the upper atmosphere to the radio spectrum

Space observation involves more than taking images of Earth from satellites. It also allows us to investigate atmospheric changes, the ionospheric environment, and the radio signals that affect navigation and communications.

The Hsia Han-Min Space Technology Center participates in Taiwan's FORMOSAT-8 national space mission, integrating scientific research with satellite engineering through scientific payload development, system integration, in-orbit verification, and observational data analysis.

From the optical and plasma instruments successfully verified in orbit aboard FS-8A to the multi-band radio-frequency environment observation system being planned for FS-8F, we develop scientific instruments and use real space missions to move technology from design into validation and application under actual operating conditions.

## FORMOSAT-8A

### Capturing atmospheric emissions from space to explore ionospheric changes

**Dual-band optical imaging × In-situ plasma measurement**

Transient flashes in the upper atmosphere and constantly changing plasma in the ionosphere may seem far removed from daily life, but they are closely connected to the satellite navigation and radio communications on which we rely.

To better understand these phenomena, the Center led the development of two scientific payloads for the first FORMOSAT-8 satellite: the Dual-band Imager for Atmospheric Transients (DIAT) and the Electron Temperature and Density Probe (TeNeP). Together, optical imaging and plasma measurements examine the space environment from complementary perspectives.

#### Dual-band Imager for Atmospheric Transients (DIAT)｜Capturing rapid changes in the upper atmosphere

DIAT combines ultraviolet and 630 nm visible-light imaging to observe energetic lightning, transient luminous events, and airglow, capturing upper-atmospheric emission features that are difficult to observe comprehensively from the ground.

The instrument integrates independently developed optical design, image sensing, modular mechanical structures, and onboard data processing to realize a compact dual-band space imaging system within strict satellite constraints on volume, mass, and power.

#### Electron Temperature and Density Probe (TeNeP)｜Measuring the plasma environment in space

With a compact, low-power design, TeNeP directly measures electron temperature and density around the satellite, providing in-situ observations of the ionospheric plasma environment.

Whereas DIAT uses optical imaging to observe large-scale atmospheric emissions, TeNeP provides local plasma characteristics at the satellite's position. Used together, the two instruments provide more diverse observational evidence for research on atmospheric emissions and the ionosphere.

#### From ground development to space operations and successful in-orbit verification

The Center's team participated in scientific payload development, satellite system integration, functional testing, and pre-launch preparation. After FS-8A was successfully launched, the team conducted initial in-orbit operation and performance verification of the instruments.

DIAT successfully acquired and transmitted ultraviolet and 630 nm visible-light images, completing its first-light test and image-readout verification. TeNeP also obtained decodable plasma measurement waveforms, confirming the performance of its electrical and data-transmission functions in the actual orbital environment.

From optical sensing and plasma measurement to scientific data processing, FS-8A's flight results demonstrate the Center's accumulated capabilities in space-science instrument development, satellite integration, and in-orbit verification.

## FORMOSAT-8F

### Monitoring radio signals from space to detect invisible interference

**Multi-band RF observation × Electromagnetic interference analysis**

Navigation positioning errors and degraded radio communication quality may be caused not only by equipment problems, but also by human-made radio-frequency interference or ionospheric disturbances.

As satellite navigation, wireless communications, and intelligent vehicles become increasingly widespread, understanding the radio-frequency environment in different regions has become essential to improving communication reliability and navigation safety.

Building on the scientific payload development experience of FS-8A, the Center is also participating in the planning of the Multi-Spectra Radio Surveillance (MSRS) receiver for the sixth FORMOSAT-8 satellite, FS-8F. This extends the scope of observation from the upper atmosphere and ionosphere to radio signals and electromagnetic interference.

#### From a single band to multiple spectra for more complete RF environment information

MSRS is planned for deployment aboard a low-Earth-orbit satellite. By receiving and scanning signals in three important bands—L, S, and C—it will observe radio-frequency activity from different sources:

- **L band｜Satellite navigation and the ionosphere:** Analyze GNSS navigation-signal interference and ionospheric plasma disturbances.
- **S band｜Satellite communications:** Monitor the signal environment and abnormal interference in satellite communication bands.
- **C band｜Critical radio communications:** Investigate RF activity and electromagnetic interference in selected communication bands.

MSRS is planned to integrate software-defined radio (SDR), radio-frequency reception, and onboard signal-processing technologies to establish background spectra, identify anomalous signals, and analyze the time, frequency, and geographic distribution of interference events.

Unlike ground monitoring systems that are constrained by station locations and terrain, a satellite can collect radio-frequency information from different regions in orbit, providing observations with greater capacity for cross-regional comparison.

Potential applications include research on satellite-navigation interference, communication-environment assessment, and space-environment monitoring, providing new observational evidence for radio-signal security and related industrial development.

#### Cross-disciplinary integration for next-generation scientific payloads

The mission is being jointly planned by the Center's research team and National Tsing Hua University, combining expertise in radio-frequency receiver development and testing.

We will contribute to scientific mission design, payload system integration, satellite mission requirements and verification, and scientific data processing and analysis, bringing together expertise from different fields to advance multi-band radio-frequency observation technology.

The team also plans to collaborate with international research partners, including Kyoto University, the University of Electro-Communications, and Japan's Electronic Navigation Research Institute, using ground-based radio observation resources to establish coordinated satellite-ground testing and signal-verification mechanisms.

Through the FS-8F mission, the Center will further extend its experience in satellite engineering and scientific data analysis while exploring new directions in spaceborne radio-frequency sensing, electromagnetic-environment monitoring, and satellite observation data applications.
